# Layer homepage offer journey refresh

`artifact_readiness: requirements-only`

## Context

The homepage hero now positions Layer around AI workflows built around the client’s business and managed after launch. The existing operating-model section delays the commercial offer, while the current four service cards mix an introductory call, an implementation project, a specialist knowledge path and an advisory relationship.

Michael has approved a simpler model: audit the opportunity, design/build/launch one workflow, then let the client choose between handover and an ongoing managed relationship.

## Intended outcome

A prospective client should understand immediately after the hero:

1. how Layer finds the right first opportunity;
2. that design, build and launch form one complete implementation engagement;
3. that a client can buy one bounded workflow and receive a handover;
4. that Layer can manage, maintain and expand the workflow through an optional retainer.

## Approved offer model

### AI Opportunity Audit

A structured review of how work moves through the business, focused on identifying where AI or automation could create credible value and which workflow should be addressed first. It produces tangible findings and a recommended next step.

The audit replaces the former AI Opportunity Session. The intended long-term offer is paid. During the initial launch, Layer may offer a limited number at no cost to selected businesses.

### AI Workflow Build

One scoped engagement covering workflow design, architecture, integration, controls, testing, production launch, stabilisation and handover. Clients buy a working outcome rather than separate design and deployment products.

### After launch: two client paths

1. **One-off delivery** — Layer stabilises, documents and hands the workflow over. No retainer is required.
2. **Managed by Layer** — Layer monitors operational health, investigates failures, maintains the agreed workflow and expands into further workflows through separately scoped phases.

## Homepage information architecture

The preferred homepage order is:

1. Hero
2. Offer journey
3. Example systems
4. Six ways Layer measures value
5. Reliability and design-for-failure reassurance
6. FAQ
7. Contact

Remove the standalone Connect, Coordinate and Control section. Preserve those ideas inside the build and management copy: connect existing tools, coordinate work and keep people in control of consequential decisions.

## Presentation requirements

- Retain Layer’s current typography, atmosphere, colour, spacing and restrained visual language.
- Present a clear sequence: **Audit the opportunity → Design, build & launch → Choose what happens next**.
- Show the post-launch choice as a genuine branch, not two consecutive mandatory steps.
- Keep deeper service explanations available through the current progressive-disclosure dialog pattern.
- Use plain-language display headings and reserve technical terms such as architecture and production deployment for supporting copy.
- Do not copy the reference site’s visual identity, stock architectural imagery, enterprise claims or department-replacement language.

## In scope

- Remove the standalone operating-model section.
- Replace the current four-service journey with the approved audit/build/branch model.
- Remove AI Knowledge Foundation from the primary homepage offer; retain it as an implementation capability where trusted knowledge is required.
- Remove AI Enablement Partner from the primary homepage offer; retain useful governance and value-review concepts within ongoing management where relevant.
- Replace homepage Opportunity Session language with AI Opportunity Audit language.
- Reconcile navigation anchors, service dialogs, contact copy and homepage structured data with the new journey.

## Constraints

- The audit must promise a tangible output and must not be presented as a renamed introductory call.
- The initial no-cost offer must be bounded and described as available to selected businesses.
- Ongoing management is optional and separately scoped.
- Management applies to Layer-built workflows or systems Layer has formally reviewed and accepted.
- Public copy must not promise 24/7 coverage, guaranteed uptime, binding response times, unlimited support or unlimited feature development.
- Monitoring should minimise unnecessary access to workflow contents.
- Human approval, permissions, auditability, manual override and safe failure behaviour remain part of the implementation promise.

## Acceptance criteria

- The offer journey is the first section after the hero.
- A visitor can explain the audit, the workflow build and both post-launch choices after reading the section once.
- Design, build and launch appear as one engagement.
- One-off delivery is visibly complete and does not look like an inferior or unfinished option.
- Ongoing management clearly supports a retainer without overpromising operational coverage.
- The former Knowledge Foundation and Enablement Partner offers no longer compete with the main homepage journey.
- Navigation and homepage structured data contain no stale references to the removed operating-model section or Opportunity Session.
- The result remains coherent at desktop and mobile widths.

## Assumptions and later commercial decisions

- Public working name for the implementation is **AI Workflow Build**.
- Public working name for the retainer is **AI Workflow Management**, while the visible branch may use the clearer heading **Managed by Layer**.
- Initial retainer boundaries, support window, escalation route, included maintenance and treatment of new feature work will be defined outside the website before public launch.
- Pricing is not part of this website change.

Implementation design remains for CE Plan. Publishing remains a separate approval gate.
